# Gallagher Bassett LinkedIn Voice Guide

Source: 12 recent posts from the Gallagher Bassett LinkedIn company page (September 2026), with 20 posts from the parent company, Gallagher, as a secondary reference. These are observed patterns, not an official brand guideline.

## Voice
- Confident, practical and professional. The company page speaks as "we" and "our".
- Enthusiasm is measured and reserved for news about people, partners or awards ("We're excited to share...", "Join us in welcoming..."). Expert and industry content is direct, with no exclamation marks.
- The focus is on what the reader gains: insights, trends, practical strategies and better outcomes for clients, customers and injured workers.

## Structure (company page)
1. Hook: a question to the reader, or a direct statement of the issue.
2. Substance: one or two sentences with the key insight or news.
3. Call to action as the last line, ending with a colon; the link follows it. For earned media coverage, the call to action names the outlet, for example "Read the full article in Business Insurance:" or "Learn more in Insurance Business:". Write it in your own words; do not copy these examples word for word.

Length: 30 to 90 words.

## People
- Name people with their title in the format "Name, Title", with an em dash when the title has two parts: "Leah Sharp, SVP — Clinical Services".
- Seniority is abbreviated: EVP, SVP, VP.
- Titles must come from the source article. The article's title may differ from the internal title, so titles are always flagged for human verification.

## Hashtags and emojis
- Company page posts: no hashtags.
- No emojis, except functional ones for event logistics (📅 ⏰).

## Lists
- Bullets (•) only when listing three or more topics.

## Vocabulary
- Frequently used: insights, trends, explore, stay ahead, practical strategies, outcomes, clients, customers and injured workers.
- Avoid: "thrilled", "game-changer", "in today's fast-paced world", "leverage", and any superlative or success claim about GB ("leading", "successful", "invaluable") that the source article does not make.

## Visual identity (reference)
Every post carries a branded visual: navy and teal background, orange-gold accents, the GB logo at the top left, a large white headline, a spokesperson headshot with a name and title bar, a "READ MORE" or "LEARN MORE" button, and quote cards with large quotation marks.

## Channels without observed examples
The sample contains no spokesperson or employee posts. Guidance for these channels is an assumption to validate with the GB marketing team:
- Spokesperson: first person, 50 to 120 words, modest. Shares one idea from the conversation and credits the outlet. Hashtags optional (0 to 3).
- Employee advocacy: 25 to 60 words, one clear takeaway. Hashtags optional (0 to 3).
